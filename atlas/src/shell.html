A LOCAL MATHEMATICS ATLAS

Explore one layer at a time.

  1. Choose a subject. Click for a description, then “Open roadmaps” to go inside.
  2. Enter a roadmap galaxy. Click once to fly into its layer constellations. Its scope, prerequisites and consumers stay available in the details panel.
  3. Explore a layer constellation. Click its heading to focus, or select a star for a definition, theorem or construction. Open the parent layer to record progress.

Search also finds individual stages. “All roadmaps” shows the complete combined map. Hover over a galaxy to highlight its immediate connections; click once to enter. Click a layer heading to focus its constellation, then select its stars for details.

Each galaxy represents a roadmap. Click it to zoom into its layers, shown as constellations. Stars inside each layer identify definitions, theorems and constructions from the roadmap. Hover over a galaxy to reveal prerequisite links; its Links tab explains the recorded layer connections. Related-plan links are optional and dotted. Pin useful roadmaps from their details to keep them in your index.

Scroll to zoom, drag the background to pan, or drag a node to rearrange it. Use “Fit” to reset the view. Escape closes the reader or details. Your browser’s Back and Forward buttons navigate between views.

Node colours run from red through amber to green. Stage colours show status; roadmap percentages count completed tracked leaf layers. An unassessed layer stays red and is labelled “Not assessed”. Recorded status reports may be older than the current source. Your local updates override those reports only in this explorer.

Use Mission status for area totals, and filter by collection or layer activity. Your pins and status changes stay in this browser.

The full roadmap texts are embedded in this file. Reading them and exploring the graph needs no internet or local server. Source links open only when you choose them.

These are mathematical plans. Stages retain their source-decomposition status; this explorer does not mark their proofs complete. Roadmap-level cycles reflect different early and late stages. The encoded stage graph is acyclic.
